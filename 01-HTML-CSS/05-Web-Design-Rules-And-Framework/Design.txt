============================================================
1. WEB DESIGN VS. WEB DEVELOPMENT
============================================================
WEB DESIGNER:
   - Creates the overall look and feel of a website
   - Focuses on: colors, typography, layout, visual hierarchy
   - Thinks about: user experience, brand identity, emotions
WEB DEVELOPER:
   - Implements the design using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript
   - Focuses on: code, functionality, performance
   - Turns the design into a working website

Both roles work together to create a great final product.

============================================================
2. WHY TAKE DESIGN SERIOUSLY?
============================================================
GOOD DESIGN:
   • Creates an immediate and lasting good impression of the brand
   • Makes users trust the brand right away
   • Increases the perceived value of the brand or product
   • Gives users exactly what they're looking for (e.g., purchasing a product or finding information)
BAD DESIGN:
   • Makes users believe the brand doesn't care about their product
   • Makes users insecure about trusting the brand
   • Makes the brand or product seem "cheap"
   • Leaves users confused and makes it hard to reach their goal

Good web design is NOT subjective or purely creative. 
Everyone can learn the basics by following a framework/system.

============================================================
3. WEB DESIGN INGREDIENTS
============================================================
These are the 10 main ingredients of good web design:
   1. Typography
   2. Colors
   3. Images / Illustrations
   4. Icons
   5. Shadows
   6. Border-radius
   7. Whitespace
   8. Visual hierarchy
   9. Visual experience
  10. Components / Layout
Design decisions for each ingredient are based on the website's PERSONALITY (see next section).

============================================================
4. OVERVIEW OF WEBSITE PERSONALITIES
============================================================
Every website has a "personality" that guides design choices.

------------------------------------------------------------
1. SERIOUS / ELEGANT
------------------------------------------------------------
   • For: Luxury and elegance
   • Typography: Thin serif typefaces
   • Colors: Golden or pastel colors
   • Images: Big, high-quality images
   • Examples: Luxury brands, high-end products

------------------------------------------------------------
2. MINIMALIST / SIMPLE
------------------------------------------------------------
   • For: Focusing on essential text content
   • Typography: Small or medium-sized sans-serif black text
   • Colors: Black, white, few colors
   • Images: Few images and icons
   • Examples: Google, Stripe, Apple

------------------------------------------------------------
3. PLAIN / NEUTRAL
------------------------------------------------------------
   • For: Design that gets out of the way
   • Typography: Neutral and small typefaces
   • Colors: Neutral colors
   • Layout: Very structured layout
   • Examples: Big corporations, government sites

------------------------------------------------------------
4. BOLD / CONFIDENT
------------------------------------------------------------
   • For: Making an impact
   • Typography: Big and bold typography
   • Colors: Big and bright colored blocks
   • Examples: Startups, creative agencies

------------------------------------------------------------
5. CALM / PEACEFUL
------------------------------------------------------------
   • For: Products and services that care
   • Typography: Soft serif headings
   • Colors: Calming pastel colors
   • Images: Matching images/illustrations
   • Examples: Wellness apps, meditation sites

------------------------------------------------------------
6. STARTUP / UPBEAT
------------------------------------------------------------
   • For: Startups and modern products
   • Typography: Medium-sized sans-serif
   • Colors: Light-grey text and backgrounds
   • Elements: Rounded elements
   • Examples: SaaS products, tech startups

------------------------------------------------------------
7. PLAYFUL / FUN
------------------------------------------------------------
   • For: Fun, creative projects
   • Typography: Creative fonts
   • Colors: Colorful and round designs
   • Elements: Hand-drawn icons, animations, fun language
   • Examples: Games, kids' products, creative brands

============================================================
TYPOGRAPHY (Most Important Part of Design)
============================================================
Definition:
   The art and technique of arranging type to make written language legible, readable, and appealing when displayed.

============================================================
SERIF VS SANS-SERIF
============================================================
SERIF (Traditional, Classic):
   - Creates a traditional/classic look and feel
   - Conveys trustworthiness
   - Good for long text
   - Examples: Merriweather, Lora, Playfair Display

SANS-SERIF (Modern, Simple):
   - Modern look and feel
   - Clean and simple
   - Easier to choose for beginner designers
   - Examples: Inter, Roboto, Open Sans, Lato

============================================================
GOLDEN RULES (Quick Summary)
============================================================
1. Use only good and popular typefaces (play it safe).
2. One typeface per page is enough! Limit to 2 max.
3. Choose typeface based on website personality:
   - Elegant → Serif
   - Modern → Sans-serif
   - Bold → Heavy typeface
4. For normal text: use 16px to 32px.
5. For long text (blog posts): 20px or bigger.
6. For headlines: 50px+ with weight 600+.
7. Don't use font weight under 400.
8. Use less than 75 characters per line.
9. Line-height for normal text: 1.5 to 2.
10. For big text: line-height below 1.5.
11. Decrease letter-spacing in headlines if it looks unnatural.
12. Usually, don't justify text.
13. Don't center long text blocks. Small blocks are fine.

COLORS
============================================================
Definition:
   Colors are one of the most powerful design ingredients.
   They convey meaning, set the mood, and guide the user's eye.

============================================================
1. CHOOSE THE RIGHT COLOR (Match Website Personality)
============================================================
Every color conveys a meaning. Choose based on your website's personality:
RED:
   - Draws a lot of attention
   - Symbolizes: power, passion, excitement

ORANGE:
   - Less aggressive than red
   - Symbolizes: happiness, cheerfulness, creativity

YELLOW:
   - Symbolizes: joy, brightness, intelligence

GREEN:
   - Symbolizes: harmony, nature, growth, health

BLUE:
   - Symbolizes: peace, trustworthiness, professionalism
   - Most common color for corporate websites

PURPLE:
   - Symbolizes: wealth, wisdom, magic

PINK:
   - Symbolizes: romance, care, affection

BROWN:
   - Symbolizes: nature, durability, comfort

BLACK:
   - Symbolizes: power, elegance, minimalism
   - But also: grief and sorrow

============================================================
2. USE A GOOD COLOR TONE
============================================================
Don't choose a random tone or CSS named colors (like "red", "blue").

Use a proper color system with consistent tones.

Tools for colors:
   - Open Color
   - Tailwind CSS Colors
   - Flat UI Colors 2
   - Palleton.com
   - Coolors.co

============================================================
3. ESTABLISH A COLOR SYSTEM
============================================================
Your color palette needs at least TWO types of colors:
   1. MAIN COLOR   → the primary brand color
   2. GREY COLOR   → for text, backgrounds, borders

Optional (with more experience):
   3. ACCENT COLOR → for highlights and special elements

For diversity, create lighter and darker versions:
   - TINTS (lighter versions)
   - SHADES (darker versions)

Example palette:
   MAIN:   teal (with tints and shades)
   ACCENT: red (with tints and shades)
   GREY:   dark blue-grey (with tints and shades)

============================================================
4. WHEN AND HOW TO USE COLORS
============================================================
Use your MAIN COLOR to draw attention to the most important elements on the page (buttons, links, highlights).

Use colors to add interesting ACCENTS or make entire components or sections stand out.

You can try to use your color strategically in images and illustrations.

============================================================
5. COLORS AND TYPOGRAPHY (Important!)
============================================================
On dark colored backgrounds, use a TINT of the background color for the text (a lighter version).

Text should usually NOT be completely black. Lighten it up — pure black looks heavy and uninviting.

Don't make text too light! Use a tool to check contrast between text and background.
   Contrast ratio rules:
      - 4.5:1 for normal text
      - 3:1 for large text (18px+)

💡 Use Coolors.co to check contrast ratios.

============================================================
KEY TAKEAWAYS
============================================================
- Color = meaning. Match it to your website's personality.
- Don't use random colors — use a proper color system.
- At minimum: 1 main color + 1 grey color.
- Create tints (lighter) and shades (darker) for diversity.
- Use main color to draw attention to important elements.
- Use accents for highlights.
- Text should not be pure black — lighten it.
- Always check contrast (4.5:1 for normal, 3:1 for large text).
- Use tools like Coolors, Tailwind Colors, Open Color.
